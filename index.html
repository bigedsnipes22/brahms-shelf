Another Library of Congress item

A /resource/ address carries the ID on its own. A bare item number needs LoC's API — if that's blocked, set a page count and missing pages drop out of the sheet.

Using it

Saving on iPad: open the image, then Share → Save to Files. Save to Photos re-encodes it and you lose the point of pulling a TIFF.

Page numbers: at the Library of Congress the sp= number in their viewer is the image number — sp=19 is image 0019. A manuscript's own written pagination is a separate sequence; for the Third Symphony image = written page + 2, checked against image 0020 carrying the hand-numbered 18. No other item here has been checked that way.

Rights: Lübeck releases its images CC0. Berlin marks its scans Public Domain Mark 1.0. The Library of Congress items are public domain. All three permit the downloads this page builds.

Known, and not online

  • Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde, Vienna — 114 Brahms music autographs, UNESCO Memory of the World, including the German Requiem, the Double Concerto, and the sketches for a fifth symphony. Their entire public digital output is concert programmes; not one manuscript. The largest single gap in Brahms sources by a wide margin.
  • Symphony No. 4 — the autograph belongs to the Allgemeine Musikgesellschaft Zürich, 138 pages with Brahms's corrections and Simrock's engraving marks. Not digitized; the 1974 six-colour facsimile is still the only way to see it.
  • British Library — four autographs (Piano Concerto No. 2 reduction, Zigeunerlieder op. 103, the canon op. 113/9, and the Rhapsody op. 119/4). Digitised, but the images have been offline since the 2023 cyber-attack.
  • Morgan Library — nine Brahms records including the Symphony No. 1 autograph (movements 2–4; the first is lost) and the Hungarian Dances. Whether page images are viewable could not be settled from here — worth one look at themorgan.org/music/manuscript/114285.
  • Violin Concerto solo part, corrected by Brahms and Joachim together — listed in LoC's research guide, but its API record 404s.

How it was built

Every identifier and page count here was read from the holding library's own API or IIIF manifest, one record at a time. Nothing was inferred from a neighbouring record's pattern. Where a library holds Brahms autographs but has not put images online, it is listed above rather than quietly omitted. LoC's own research guide lists the Op. 118 / Op. 119 intermezzi as catalogue-only; they are in fact digitized, and they are on this shelf.
